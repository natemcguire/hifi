HIFI Live.

Six rooms, on all day and all night.

Each stream is the same mid-90s den: wood paneling, a lava lamp, the station’s HIFI TV clips on a CRT and a record turning in front. The names, colors and clips are the stations you already have in HIFI, and every song is ours.

Before any of this goes live

Only our own music

Dial-Up, Shareware Summer, Night Build and Pixel Drift already play HIFI Studio tracks (procedural compositions plus Nate’s AI-generated seeds), so they are ready for a stream.

In the app, Lo-Fi Beats to Work To and Fireside Christmas relay SomaFM. Their YouTube streams can’t use that audio: each needs its own generated catalog first. No licensed or third-party tracks anywhere.

Christmas: public-domain carols only

Fireside Christmas plays our own AI-generated acoustic versions of compositions in the public domain: Silent Night, O Holy Night, Jingle Bells, Deck the Halls, The First Noel, God Rest Ye Merry Gentlemen, What Child Is This?, Hark! The Herald Angels Sing, Joy to the World, O Come All Ye Faithful, Good King Wenceslas, We Wish You a Merry Christmas.

Modern songs are still under copyright, and a cover needs a license even when we generate it ourselves: All I Want for Christmas Is You, Last Christmas, White Christmas, Rockin’ Around the Christmas Tree, Have Yourself a Merry Little Christmas, Let It Snow!, Winter Wonderland (until 2030). On YouTube that means a sync license too, and Content ID would claim them.

Use the old lyrics and our own arrangements: many modern arrangements and some English translations are copyrighted even when the tune isn’t.

The room

The CRT plays each station’s four HIFI TV clips (the same files the app uses), one per song, under scanlines, noise, a VHS tracking band and a little wobble. The lava lamp and the record label take the station’s color.

The turntable here is drawn in CSS. For the real stream, Guillermo Rauch’s groove-level record player sim (rauchg/vinyl, MIT, keep its notice) could render the deck.
