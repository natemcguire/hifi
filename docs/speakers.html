HIFI on other home speakers

Where a free live-radio app can get on Google, Apple, Sonos and the rest, what it costs and what the Alexa skill already gives us. Researched 19 November 2026.

At a glance

  • Apple is the open door. HomePod plays any iPhone app that answers Siri's media requests (SiriKit INPlayMediaIntent). There is no partner program any more, so "Hey Siri, play Z100 on HIFI" can work on HomePod in about a week, using the app we already ship.
  • AirPlay already works. The iPhone and iPad app sets the long-form audio policy and fill in Now Playing, so HIFI already plays on HomePod, Apple TV and AirPlay 2 Sonos speakers from the phone. Only a quick check is needed.
  • Google voice is closed. Conversational Actions shut down in June 2023. Radio by voice on Nest now comes only from Google's media partners (TuneIn, iHeart and the like), with no application form. Google Cast is open: casting from the app and web player to Nest speakers and Hubs costs $5 and about a week.
  • Sonos is possible but slow. The Sonos Music API partner process takes four to six months of review and preview. Our rebroadcast of stations like Z100 may be questioned; the house stations are the safer start.
  • Skip the dead and closed ones: Gemini for Home, Sonos Voice Control, Alexa built into Sonos and Bose speakers, Bixby, Meta Portal and Cortana.
  • Reuse: the catalog (stations.json), the now-playing feed and the station ranker carry over everywhere. The ranker is the piece worth porting to Swift (Siri) or serving from the Railway backend (Sonos).

Apple: HomePod, Siri, Apple TV, CarPlay

AirPlay 2 (works now)

The app already sets AVAudioSession to .playback with the .longFormAudio policy and fills in MPNowPlayingInfoCenter and MPRemoteCommandCenter (ios/Sources/Radio.swift). That is what HomePod, Apple TV and AirPlay 2 Sonos speakers need. To check on a device: the station name and art on the HomePod's Now Playing, and play, pause and skip from Control Center. An AirPlay button (AVRoutePickerView) in the player would make it easier to find; today it is only in Control Center.

Siri on HomePod and iPhone

Status
Open. Since WWDC23, any app that supports SiriKit Media Intents is supported on HomePod. HomePod forwards the request to the owner's iPhone, which starts HIFI and plays to the HomePod. Since HomePod 17.4 Siri learns which app you use; there is no default-service picker.
Route
An Intents extension handling INPlayMediaIntent: resolve "Z100", "jazz" or "something for work" to a station, then play in the app (handleInApp). Add App Shortcuts (AudioPlaybackIntent) for "Hey Siri, play HIFI".
Effort
3–5 days, plus 1–2 for App Shortcuts.
Hurdles
Normal App Store review. The app must be on the owner's iPhone on the same Wi-Fi, with Recognize My Voice on; the first request asks for Siri permission; the app must answer within 10 seconds.
Reuse
The station ranker (alexa/lambda/search.js, which already mirrors the app's StationSearch) for names, tags, genres and folded numbers; the catalog; "play HIFI" resumes the last station as on Alexa.

Not confirmed: whether Apple's newer App Intents audio schema replaces SiriKit on HomePod in iOS 27. SiriKit media intents are not marked deprecated in Apple's documentation today.

CarPlay and Apple TV

CarPlay needs the free carplay-audio entitlement (a form; days to approve) and a station list in CarPlay's templates, 3–5 days. Apple TV is a tvOS target reusing the Swift player, 4–8 days. Both reuse the catalog; Siri on Apple TV opens the app through the same media intents.

Google: Nest speakers, Nest Hub, Android

Voice on Nest (closed)

Google shut down Conversational Actions on 13 June 2023 and pointed radio stations to aggregators. Spoken radio requests on Nest now come from Media Actions feeds (schema.org RadioBroadcastService, which does accept internet-only stations), but Google works with "a limited number of providers at a time" and has no application form. Gemini for Home uses the same media partners and has no developer route that we could find.

Google Cast (open)

Route
The Cast SDK in the iPhone app and the Web Sender SDK in the web player. The Default Media Receiver plays MP3, AAC and HLS with no registration; a styled receiver for the Nest Hub screen costs $5 once, with "supports audio-only devices" ticked.
Effort
3–6 days for both.
Hurdles
No review. The iPhone asks for local-network access.
Reuse
The catalog's stream URLs and the now-playing feed as Cast metadata. The station art for the Echo Show (/alexa/covers/, /alexa/backgrounds/) suits the Nest Hub as is.

Android app

The one route to "Hey Google, play jazz on HIFI" without a partnership is an Android app with a Media3 MediaLibraryService: Assistant sends the spoken search to the app (onPlayFromSearch), and the same service makes HIFI an Android Auto app. It is a new Kotlin app, 2–4 weeks, $25 to Google Play, and a personal Play account must first run a 12-tester, 14-day closed test (an organisation account for McGuire LLC is reportedly exempt; not confirmed by Google). The ranker, catalog, state and now-playing all carry over.

Sonos

Status
Open on paper, slow in practice. A service joins through the Sonos Music API (SMAPI): register as a partner, build against the developer portal sandbox, then roughly 6 weeks of application review, 6 of validation and 6–8 of preview before it is listed. No fee is stated.
Route
A SMAPI (SOAP) service, which could be another route on the Railway service that runs the Alexa skill. HIFI needs no sign-in, which keeps it simple.
Effort
5–8 days to build browse, search and now-playing; then months of process.
Hurdles
Brand assets, an FAQ page, an SLA during preview. Sonos reviews content rights, so relaying broadcasters such as Z100 or KIIS FM may be questioned; Radio Paradise, SomaFM and HIFI's own synth stations are the safer opening set. The old local "customSD" test route no longer works on S2.
Reuse
High: the catalog becomes the browse tree, the ranker becomes search, stations.json gives stream URLs and now-playing.

Sonos Voice Control only plays a closed list of services (Sonos Radio, Apple Music, Amazon Music, Spotify and a few others). AirPlay 2 Sonos speakers already get HIFI from the iPhone.

Amazon beyond the Echo

  • Fire TV, Echo Hub, every Echo Show: already covered by the skill, including the HIFI TV screen.
  • Alexa built into Sonos, Bose and other brands: reports say third-party audio skills don't play there, and Amazon has closed its Alexa Built-in developer tools. Not confirmed for 2026; one test on a friend's Sonos One would settle it.
  • Alexa+: nothing published yet on how custom AudioPlayer skills behave under Alexa+. Worth a test on an Alexa+ Echo.

Others

  • Roku: a SceneGraph channel with certification, 5–10 days, no voice reuse.
  • TuneIn and other directories: only HIFI's own synth stations would qualify, and only as a continuous stream rather than MP3 files. A listing would make "play HIFI Synth on TuneIn" work on Google, Alexa and Sonos, under TuneIn's name. US stations paid $249 a quarter in 2022; current terms not confirmed.
  • Dead: Samsung's Galaxy Home never shipped widely and Bixby has no developer activity since 2019; Meta Portal ended in 2022; Cortana skills ended in 2021.

Recommended order

  1. Check AirPlay on a HomePod (under a day). It already works on paper; confirm art and controls, and add an AirPlay button to the player.
  2. Siri media intents in the iOS app (about a week). The only open voice route on a major speaker other than Alexa: "Hey Siri, play Z100 on HIFI" on iPhone and HomePod.
  3. Google Cast in the app and web player (about a week, $5). The only open way onto Nest speakers and Hubs.
  4. CarPlay (about a week). Free entitlement, same catalog and intents.
  5. Apply to Sonos early, then build the SMAPI service on Railway while the review runs. Start with the house stations.
  6. Android app with Android Auto (2–4 weeks), when Google voice and cars are worth a new app.
  7. Roku and Apple TV, nice to have.
